PCH Winner Notification Scam: How to Verify a Real Prize

Publishers Clearing House is a real, long-established sweepstakes operator—and precisely because the name is familiar, scammers frequently impersonate it. A PCH logo, caller ID, social profile, or claim that a Prize Patrol is coming does not prove the message is genuine.

By SafeTracker Editorial Team · Updated August 18, 2026 · How this research is produced

Save this checklist

Quick check

  • Real PCH prizes do not require an advance payment to claim.
  • Do not send gift cards, cryptocurrency, wires, or payment-app transfers.
  • Verify the promotion through PCH’s official website and published support channels.
  • Do not trust a phone number or link supplied only by the person contacting you.

Common PCH impersonation tactics

Scammers may call, text, email, or send social-media messages claiming that you won a large cash prize, vehicle, or recurring payment. They often create urgency and say a payment is needed for taxes, processing, insurance, delivery, or prize activation.

Another tactic is to request sensitive personal or banking information before providing independently verifiable rules, drawing information, or administrator details.

  • The message says you must keep the win secret.
  • A courier or prize representative supposedly cannot arrive until you pay.
  • The sender asks you to buy gift cards and read the numbers aloud.
  • You are told to use a particular bank, payment app, or cryptocurrency kiosk.

How to verify a PCH notification

Stop communicating through the suspicious message. Open PCH’s official website independently, locate its fraud guidance or support information, and ask whether the named promotion and notification are genuine.

Compare the promotion name, entry period, drawing date, eligibility, and prize with the official rules. If the details do not match, treat the contact as fraudulent.
