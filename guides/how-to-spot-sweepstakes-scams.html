How to Spot a Sweepstakes Scam

A real sweepstakes can still generate marketing, but it will not require you to pay a fee to claim a prize. The fastest way to evaluate a message is to slow down, identify the actual sponsor, and verify the promotion outside the message that contacted you.

By SafeTracker Editorial Team · Updated September 03, 2026 · How this research is produced

Save this checklist

Quick check

  • Never pay taxes, shipping, processing, insurance, or activation fees to claim a prize.
  • Do not trust caller ID, a social-media profile, or a logo as proof of identity.
  • Find the sponsor’s official website independently and use its published contact information.
  • Do not provide bank credentials, gift-card numbers, crypto, or remote access to your device.

The clearest warning signs

Advance-fee demands are the strongest signal. Scammers often describe the payment as taxes, customs, delivery, insurance, or a refundable deposit. Legitimate sponsors do not collect prize taxes by gift card, wire transfer, cryptocurrency, or payment app.

Urgency is another common tactic. A caller may claim the prize expires today or instruct you to keep the win secret. That pressure is designed to prevent independent verification.

  • A message says you won a promotion you do not remember entering.
  • The sender uses a free email account or a look-alike domain.
  • You must pay, purchase, or complete offers before receiving the prize.
  • The sender requests sensitive information before proving the promotion exists.
  • The message contains no official rules, sponsor name, or verifiable drawing details.

Verify the win independently

Do not use the phone number or link in the winning message as your only verification source. Search for the sponsor’s official website, locate the promotion’s rules or winners page, and contact the sponsor through information published there.

Compare the promotion name, entry period, drawing date, eligibility, and administrator with the message. A real brand can be impersonated, so brand recognition alone is not enough.

What a legitimate winner may be asked to provide

A verified winner may need to confirm identity, eligibility, age, address, and tax information. Higher-value prizes often require an affidavit or tax form. The sequence matters: first verify the promotion and administrator, then provide only the information required through a confirmed channel.

If anything does not match the official rules, stop and contact the sponsor directly.
